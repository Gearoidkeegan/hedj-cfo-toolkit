## Short

Where the company chooses to apply hedge accounting, it does so under its reporting framework's
own rules, designating and documenting each qualifying hedge relationship at the outset, not
after the fact.

## Full

Hedge accounting is not something this policy requires: it is an accounting treatment the
company may apply to a qualifying hedge under the rules of its own reporting framework, where
doing so better reflects the economic effect of the hedge in its financial statements. The
company's reporting framework, not this policy, sets the conditions a hedge relationship must
meet.

Where the company chooses to apply hedge accounting to a hedging transaction, it designates the
hedge relationship, the hedged item and the hedging instrument, and prepares the documentation
its reporting framework requires, at the outset of the hedge rather than at a later reporting
date. A hedge that is not designated in this way is accounted for on whatever basis its
reporting framework otherwise prescribes.

The decision to apply hedge accounting to a hedge, and the documentation supporting it, is made
and documented by the finance director before the first reporting period it affects, for the
company's auditors to review, and is reviewed under the effectiveness testing clause for as long
as the hedge relationship continues.
