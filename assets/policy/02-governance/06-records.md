## Short

The company keeps a record of every treasury transaction and decision: what was done, who
authorised it, and under what authority. Records are kept for the period required by law and can
be produced to the board or an auditor on request.

## Full

The company keeps a record of every treasury transaction and treasury decision: the instrument
or facility used, the amount, the exposure it relates to, the person who executed it, the person
who approved it, and the delegated authority relied on. Records are kept in a form that lets the
board or an auditor reconstruct, for any transaction, why it was entered into and who authorised
it.

Records include correspondence and confirmations with counterparties, minutes of board decisions
on treasury matters, the register of exceptions, and the record of training completed under the
training and competence clause. Records are retained for the period required by law and by the
company's own document retention policy, whichever is longer, and are kept accessible to the
finance director, the board and the company's auditors throughout that period.

A change to how records are kept, or to the systems used to keep them, is itself recorded,
together with how existing records were carried across.
