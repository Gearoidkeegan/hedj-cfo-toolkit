## Short

The company measures each exposure by comparing the forecast or committed amount in a currency
or rate basis against the amount already covered by a hedge, a fixed rate, or a matching asset
or liability, expressed in the company's reporting currency. The same method is used
consistently from one period to the next.

## Full

Foreign exchange exposure is measured, for each currency, as the forecast or committed net
amount receivable or payable in that currency, translated into the company's reporting currency
at the rate used for that purpose in the company's accounting policies, and compared against the
amount already covered by a hedge or a matching asset or liability in the same currency.

Interest rate exposure is measured as the proportion of the company's interest-bearing debt and
cash exposed to a change in floating rates, rather than fixed for the period concerned.
Commodity exposure, where it arises, is measured on the same basis as foreign exchange exposure,
by reference to the unit or volume exposed rather than its value alone.

Measurement uses the same method, the same sources of forecast data and the same exchange and
interest rates from one reporting period to the next, so that the results reported to the board
are comparable over time. Any change to the measurement method is itself reported to the board,
together with its effect on the figures already reported.
